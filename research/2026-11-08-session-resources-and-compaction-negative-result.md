# Session resources and a compaction acceptance failure

Two November 8 first-party updates sharpen different integration boundaries. Mario Zechner's Pi p3 documents select session resources as the proposed authority boundary. OpenClaw collaborator Ayaan Zaidi (`obviyus`) reports that a landed summary-budget fix fails its later-used-identifier acceptance criterion. These are a design decision and an external measurement report, respectively. This project ran no native suite, model or scientific experiment for either update.

## Pi: the session is the proposed authority unit

Mario's [decision commit](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/commit/306fdf536282a96947dfbec636e33174d9a47092), authored at 07:23:41 UTC, drops the paper review's per-run-origin authority requirement: everyone permitted to send in a session can use its selected resources, regardless of who started the run. Its direct child [e49b1495](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/commit/e49b1495045f06643a7cc4c93330fabc2ab37ce4), authored at 07:25:00 UTC, adds a resource code sketch and a paper review of the data model. These are fixed Pi p3 documentation commits, not a release or an implemented authority guarantee.

The [resource sketch](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/blob/e49b1495045f06643a7cc4c93330fabc2ab37ce4/apps/p3/docs/resources-code/book.md) explicitly says **“Not decided, not built.”** Plugins contribute include/restrict selectors; their live server-side composition supplies per-session views. The proposed worker principal names the session. Server-run MCP/search/mail calls check its current selection, while model-provider request credentials are pushed to workers with expiry and refreshed on the server. The examples omit parts of selector lifecycle and use proposed names/helpers. Resource removal, already-started operations, retained credential values and multi-user execution remain separate contracts to resolve.

The accompanying “model stress” report starts **“Written on paper, without building anything.”** Here “model” means the resource/data model. It is not an ML-model experiment or an executed load test. References to existing p2 behavior and line-count estimates remain the authors' analysis; this project did not audit those p2 source paths. The owner decision deliberately drops the report's per-run-authority gap. That proposal does not change our pinned local runtime, current-permission requirements or the user's authorization rules.

Actual future caller: the p3 worker's Chord/Durable resource view and tool/provider registries. At many sessions, selector recomputation, server-side delivery and cancellation of in-flight operations may add cost, but no workload ceiling or performance was measured. A justified integration must first pin the implemented revision and authority scope, then preregister multi-session arrivals, admission/backpressure, revocation during calls, cancellation, idempotent effects and two process restarts. Report successful tasks and denominators with effective throughput, end-to-end/tail latency, measured peak resources/communication and observation cost. Retain a rollback interface. This is a future gate, not a new protocol or reopened fixture.

## OpenClaw: preserving the generated summary is insufficient

[PR 195028](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/pull/195028), by `obviyus`, merged at 06:08:12 UTC as [2d4ec7bf](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/commit/2d4ec7bf48c91a42753e698c6a2f77dad5dcd766). Its reported repair prioritizes the generated body over verbatim preserved tool turns within the same 16,000-character cap. The development replay used 16 compactions; those results concern model-chosen IDs, decision/TODO bullets and a different population from the later acceptance report. The author's Gateway child scenario used a mock provider. None of these tests ran here.

The author posted an [acceptance bar](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/issues/194408#issuecomment-6718238417) at 06:28:56 UTC and a [failure report](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/issues/194408#issuecomment-6718738842) at 07:27:54 UTC. Both public texts and their ordering are verified. The author calls the bar preregistered before post-fix measurement; public timestamps alone do not independently establish when measurement began or prove its execution followed a frozen protocol.

| Author-reported held-out result | Fixed bar / interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 20 compactions: 10 hamverbot, 10 recent peanutto | Separate from 16 development compactions |
| Later-used identifier retention 46.5% → 46.5%, +0.0 percentage points | Requires at least +3 points; fails |
| At least as good as main in 20/20 cases | Satisfies the separate ≥80% condition; does not rescue the failed gain criterion |
| 23 identifiers used next but missing from the fixed summary: 13 lost before the summarizer, 10 omitted by its model, 0 cut by the 16k cap | A loss-location breakdown, not the denominator of 46.5% |

The author explicitly concludes **fail**. The full identifier denominator and aggregation method are not disclosed, and this project has no raw held-out traces, cached responses, seeds or scoring implementation to reproduce the percentages. Supporting chained replay improves from 11/68 to 25/68 identifiers over three sessions with three compactions each. Thirty blind questions score 40/60 → 46/60; 60 is the reported score denominator, not a question count. The shortened-output check is provisional, only 0/5 examined results. These external reports do not establish general quality or statistical reliability.

The online seven-day before/after deployment window is **not executed**: the author says the fix is not deployed yet. The offline marker result 13/20 = 65% is not an after-deployment rate; re-touch has before baselines only. Merged code is not online acceptance.

Our inference is that a repaired final-budget boundary can leave the downstream information-use objective unchanged when loss occurs during sampling or summarization. For an actual compaction runner, a separately justified study should pin source-span identity, sampling, summary and retained-tail versions; count eligible downstream identifiers and successful tasks at each boundary; bind retrieval/retry/restart budgets; and measure complete cost and resources under a fixed cap. The author's deterministic ID index/transcript pointer and chunked summarization are proposals, not implementations validated here. Increasing the cap is not supported as a remedy for these 23 missing identifiers.
