# Wide CP state summaries: a concrete cost signal, with numerical limits

[FLA issue #1683](https://github.com/fla-org/flash-linear-attention/issues/1683), opened by `NuojCheng` on 7 November 2026 at 04:20:22 UTC, reports a sharp cost increase in context-parallel (CP) state summaries at key dimension 256. We retrieved the complete issue and reviewed both complete kernel source files at the reported main revision, resolved to [`fbe2c383c191cdbdd7656864305c9eadee1a6756`](https://github.com/fla-org/flash-linear-attention/commit/fbe2c383c191cdbdd7656864305c9eadee1a6756). The public repository and the captured source carry the MIT license. This is a new issue and a source review, not a released fix, our reproduction or a training result. The reporter offers to submit their own change and tests; we have not implemented a competing patch.

The concrete callers are `chunk_cp_fwd_h` and `chunk_cp_bwd_dh`. They write local affine state summaries, respectively `h_out = M @ h_in + h_local` and `dh_in = dM @ dh_out + dh_local`. In the reviewed wrappers, `BK` is the next power of two of the key dimension, `BV` is 64 for key dimensions above 64, and the grid separates local-state columns from transform columns. The local-state branches already use 64-row strips. The transform branches construct square `BK × BK` diagonal, product and transition tensors, then compose that transition with a `BK × BV` FP32 transform tile for each chunk. These source-level tensor shapes corroborate a concrete materialization mechanism. They do not establish which arrays the compiler keeps live, physical register use or actual spills.

The reporter's public baseline script calls the state functions on one A100-SXM4-80GB without `torch.distributed`, using a 16,384-token shard, one head, value dimension 256 and chunk size 64. The reported environment is FLA 0.6.0/main, Torch 2.15.0+cu132, Triton 3.9.0 and Python 3.12.13. The following numbers are entirely reporter measurements; we did not run this script or verify the hardware.

| Baseline quantity | K=128 | K=256 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Forward warm kernel time | 4.33 ms | 228.52 ms |
| Backward warm kernel time | 3.60 ms | 251.58 ms |
| Forward first call | 65 s | 909 s |
| Backward first call | 70 s | 936 s |
| Selected forward spill diagnostic, as reported | 478 B | 10,394 B |
| Selected backward spill diagnostic, as reported | 404 B | 9,364 B |

First call includes compilation, autotuning and execution. The spill diagnostic comes from the reporter's Triton cache inspection; it is neither total traffic nor peak allocated/reserved GPU memory. The issue also reports that every tested K=256 candidate spills heavily or exceeds shared-memory capacity. We have not inspected generated IR or compiler metadata. The kernel-time ratios do not establish end-to-end training throughput, communication cost, or the issue's claimed dominance at 64K+ tokens per rank. Nor do we adopt the issue's approximate fourfold work estimate: the full tiled recurrence and grid must be counted before making an operation-count claim.

The proposed change reassociates `(D - K^T W) M` as `D M - K^T (W M)`, where `D M` is diagonal row scaling, and strips the transform state into 64-row pieces. The backward proposal uses `D M - W^T (K M)`. The DPLR/`USE_BG` path uses the corresponding plus sign and gated `bg` operand. In exact arithmetic this exposes a way to avoid explicitly forming the square transition. It also changes floating-point evaluation order. A shared `CP_DOT_PRECISION` setting alone cannot prove numerical equivalence.

For their local fixed four-warp/two-stage strip version, the reporter gives 7.6 ms for each K=256 kernel versus 231.6/254.3 ms for the autotuned baseline, and 9.7/10.3 s first calls versus 926/979 s. These are roughly 30×/33× warm-kernel ratios in that separate table. The full proposed patch is absent from the captured issue, so these figures are not independently reproducible from its baseline script. Fixed versus autotuned configuration also confounds any attribution of cold-start improvement solely to reassociation or strip layout.

The numerical check uses a different workload: 2,048 tokens, two heads and weak decay. The reporter describes bit-identical `h`/`dh`, but relative L2 differences in `M`/`dM` of 1.2e-5 at K=128 and 2.4e-5 at K=256. This is evidence of numerical difference, not bitwise transform equivalence, a full-gradient guarantee or acceptable long-chain/model quality. Strong decay in a timing workload can suppress a transform discrepancy; weak-decay, varied-length and complete forward/backward controls matter separately.

Our engineering inference is narrow: a recurrent operator's mathematical storage story can miss a shape-dependent transition materialization and cold-autotune cost at a real native caller. CP affine summaries are distinct from our H1 residual bank. This report gives no evidence that our bank improves quality, memory or training speed, and does not repair the saved negative H1 training outcome.

If a justified training caller later needs this optimization, the integration point is the existing transform branches, with an explicit fallback to the pinned baseline. Before execution, a separate protocol must fix the public patch, shapes, gate variants, dtype/precision, seeds, hardware/software, cold/warm policy and resource ceilings. Acceptance would need predeclared numerical/full-gradient and held-out quality tolerances, useful end-to-end throughput, step/tail time, peak device resources, communication and observation costs, including cold compilation/autotuning. A one-GPU kernel check and an actual multi-rank training comparison would remain different validation levels. No such protocol or experiment is admitted here, and no new observer prerequisite is created. Existing H1 negatives, finite H2/evaluation gates, and H3/H4 unmet gates remain unchanged.
